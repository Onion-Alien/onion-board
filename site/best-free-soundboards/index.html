The best free soundboards for Windows in 2026

A soundboard plays clips into your voice chat when you press a key. Here's how the main Windows options compare, and which one suits you.

We made one of these. This page is written by the maker of Onion Board, so read it with that in mind. We checked the other apps on their own websites in November 2026, and we say where they beat ours. Prices and features can change, so check before you rely on one.

The short answer

  • Only use Discord, and only need a few short clips? Discord's own soundboard. There's nothing to install.
  • Want sounds in Discord and games, free, with no virtual cable? Onion Board.
  • Mostly want lots of ready-made voices? Voicemod.
  • Want something small and simple, and don't mind setting up a cable? Resanance.
  • Happy to pay once for a long-running app? Soundpad.
  • On Linux as well as Windows? Soundux.

Each one in a few lines

Onion Board

Free and open source, with no account and no ads. Your sounds go straight into the mic you already use, so Discord and your games keep their normal mic setting. It also has a voice changer, text-to-speech, an in-game overlay and search for sounds on YouTube and SoundCloud.

Where it falls short: Windows warns you when you install it, because it isn't code-signed. Putting sounds into your mic asks for admin rights. The download is about 100 MB, big for a soundboard. And with this much in it, there's more to look through than in a simple app.

Discord's soundboard

Already in Discord, so it's the easiest start. Everyone in the voice channel hears your clip. It's made for brief clips, a server starts with 8 slots and boosting raises that to 48. It doesn't reach in-game voice chat or other apps. Using one server's sounds in another needs Nitro.

Voicemod

The big name, and its main strength is voices: it advertises more than 200, plus AI voices and a phone app to control it. It installs a virtual mic that you pick in each app. The download is free, with more behind a paid plan. How it compares with Onion Board.

Resanance

A simple free soundboard: as many sounds and hotkeys as you want, per-sound volume, text-to-speech and MIDI pads. You send it to a virtual cable, then pick that cable as your mic in each app. There's no voice changer. How it compares with Onion Board.

EXP Soundboard

A small, older app for the basics: hotkeys and two outputs. It needs Java and a virtual cable, and it looks quiet these days, so expect some rough edges on a new PC. How it compares with Onion Board.

Soundux

Open source and one of the few with Linux support, where it can send sound to one chosen app. On Windows it uses VB-CABLE. Its page says a major rewrite is under way, so check its state before you settle on it.

Soundpad

Not free, but cheap, and it has been around for years. It plays straight into your voice chat with no cable, and it can record sounds too. It has no voice changer. How it compares with Onion Board.

Questions

  • What's a virtual cable? A pretend audio device that carries sound from one app to another. It works, but you have to pick it as your mic in every app. How to do without one.
  • Can I keep my sounds if I switch? Your sound files are yours, so you can drag them into any app. Onion Board can also bring your board over from Soundpad, Resanance, Soundux or EXP Soundboard.
  • Why does Discord cut my sounds off? Its noise filter can treat them as background noise. Our Discord setup guide shows which settings to change.
